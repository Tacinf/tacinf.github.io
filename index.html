Abstract

Force-aware manipulation requires a robot to infer appropriate interaction forces from task context and tactile feedback. Tacinf is a low-cost modular infrastructure that predicts actions and fingertip forces from language, vision, and touch, with an optional language-conditioned force level for explicit force regulation. It combines simulation data synthesis and a tactile benchmark (Tabero), policy learning (VTLA), handheld data collection with a shared deployment gripper (Tacumi), and task-aligned simulation-to-real evaluation (Taco). On Taco’s six Pick and Place tasks, the eight policy variants in the paper yield Pearson r=0.998 and MMRV=0.010 between simulated and real-robot mean success rates. With both object positions and instances randomized, mean real-robot success rises from 20% with VR teleoperation alone to 60% with VR, simulation, and Tacumi UMI data.

04 / Data pipeline

From handheld collection to robot learning.

A common data schema connects handheld collection with teleoperation, simulation, and policy training.

  1. 01
    Guide & feel

    Move the device through the task and command the opening with the trigger. Gripper-motor load is returned as active resistance.

  2. 02
    Track & record

    Map tracked controller poses to the gripper tool center and record visual, tactile, and motor streams against a shared host-time reference.

  3. 03
    Train & deploy

    Combine demonstrations through the common schema, then execute with the reused gripper drive and command/state interface.

Calibration and signal alignment
Controller → tool center

A fixed controller-to-tool transform converts controller poses into gripper tool-center poses in the Quest tracking frame. Calibrate this relationship again when the mounting geometry changes.

A shared time reference

Pose, images, tactile observations, and motor states carry timestamps from the collection host. These streams have different native sampling rates; a shared clock does not imply simultaneous physical acquisition.

Tabero · ICML 2026 / Benchmark + force-aware VTLA

Tabero.

Learning gentle manipulation with closed-loop force feedback from vision, touch, and language.

Tabero turns open-source manipulation trajectories into vision–tactile–language tasks in Isaac Lab with Taxim/FOTS. Its force-aware VTLA pairs a decoupled force–position interface with a fixed hybrid controller, cutting average grip force by over 70% under gentle instructions while preserving task success.

Policy

Tacinf–VTLA.

Vision–language–action, with force as a first-class output.

A modular tactile interface for foundation VLA models. Marker-field, tactile-image, and 6-D force adapters feed a shared backbone; the policy predicts pose and fingertip-force setpoints, and a hybrid controller tracks both. Force is decoupled from position, so language can set how hard to hold without rewriting the motion.
