Abstract

Force-aware manipulation requires a robot to infer appropriate interaction forces from task context and tactile feedback. Tacinf is a low-cost modular infrastructure that predicts actions and fingertip forces from language, vision, and touch, with an optional language-conditioned force level for explicit force regulation. It combines simulation data synthesis and a tactile benchmark (Tabero), policy learning (VTLA), handheld data collection with a shared deployment gripper (Tacumi), and task-aligned simulation-to-real evaluation (Taco). Across eight policy variants, simulation preserves real-robot success ordering (Pearson r=0.998, MMRV=0.010). Mixed teleoperation, simulation, and Tacumi training raises success from 20% to 60% on position-randomized out-of-distribution objects.

Data synergy · three-source co-training

Three sources.
Complementary strengths.

Three complementary streams balance collection cost and capability: simulation scales visual and object diversity, Tacumi expands where and how objects are grasped, and teleoperation anchors demonstrations in real sensor and actuator statistics.

The system

One loop.
Four modules.

Together, the modules support tactile data synthesis and collection, force-aware policy learning, and task-aligned sim-to-real evaluation.

Tacinf–Tacumi · Hardware and collection

Tacumi.

A handheld interface that records the same gripper the robot will deploy.

The operator’s grip drives a QDD parallel gripper; a servo renders the slave motor’s torque back to the hand. Xense tactile fingertips and a wrist camera record contact as it happens. On the robot, the same gripper, sensors, and motor mount unchanged.

Tabero · ICML 2026 / Benchmark + force-aware VTLA

Tabero.

Learning gentle manipulation with closed-loop force feedback from vision, touch, and language.

Tabero turns open-source manipulation trajectories into vision–tactile–language tasks in Isaac Lab with Taxim/FOTS. Its force-aware VTLA pairs a decoupled force–position interface with a fixed hybrid controller, cutting average grip force by over 70% under gentle instructions while preserving task success.

Tacinf–VTLA · Policy

Tacinf–VTLA.

Vision–language–action, with force as a first-class output.

A modular tactile interface for foundation VLA models. Marker-field, tactile-image, and 6-D force adapters feed a shared backbone; the policy predicts pose and fingertip-force setpoints, and a hybrid controller tracks both. Force is decoupled from position, so language can set how hard to hold without rewriting the motion.

Tacinf–Taco · Sim-to-real

Taco.

A simulator that earns its seat next to the real robot.

Taco aligns the workspace, objects, robot, cameras, and tactile response for paired simulation and real-robot evaluation. It also supports mixed-data policy post-training across teleoperation, simulation, and Tacumi demonstrations.
