Tacinf–Tacumi · Hardware and collection

Tacumi.

A handheld interface that records the same gripper the robot will deploy.

The operator’s grip drives a QDD parallel gripper; a servo renders the slave motor’s torque back to the hand. Xense tactile fingertips and a wrist camera record contact as it happens. On the robot, the same gripper, sensors, and motor mount unchanged.

  • A
    Torque-based hybrid control

    Position control before contact; a closed torque-feedback loop during contact, with no external six-axis sensor.

  • B
    Tracked hand pose

    A consumer VR tracker supplies 6-DoF trajectories without an optical motion-capture rig.

  • C
    Shared gripper assembly

    Collection and deployment share geometry, occlusion, shear response, and kinematics.

  • D
    Synchronized streams

    RGB, stereo tactile, marker fields, and device motion on one timeline.

Tacinf–Tabero · Simulation and benchmark

Tabero.

A tactile benchmark built by replaying trajectories the community already trusts.

Open-source manipulation trajectories are reconstructed in Isaac Lab and replayed with a tactile gripper at ordered force levels. Each rollout carries language, RGB, tactile images, marker fields, and 6-D contact force at 20 Hz — and is scored on gentleness, not only success.

  • A
    Data reuse

    LIBERO / MuJoCo trajectories replay in Isaac Lab; average success moves 0.85 → 0.76 with the matched robot.

  • B
    Cross-modal acquisition

    Third-person, wrist, tactile, and marker-field streams stay synchronized through replay.

  • C
    Language-conditioned force levels

    100% firm versus 25% / 10% gentle; adverbs map onto a continuous force axis, not a binary switch.

  • D
    Metrics beyond success

    Average and maximum grip force plus applied force make interaction quality measurable.

Tacinf–VTLA · Policy

Tacinf–VTLA.

Vision–language–action, with force as a first-class output.

A modular tactile interface for foundation VLA models. Marker-field, tactile-image, and 6-D force adapters feed a shared backbone; the policy predicts pose and fingertip-force setpoints, and a hybrid controller tracks both. Force is decoupled from position, so language can set how hard to hold without rewriting the motion.

  • A
    Fingertip-force decomposition

    Internal grip and applied TCP wrench are separated through a two-contact mapping.

  • B
    Hybrid controller

    Feedforward plus admittance; both are needed for accurate force tracking.

  • C
    Modular tactile policy

    Marker-field, tactile-image, and force adapters on π₀ / π₀.₅ backbones with LoRA.

  • D
    Supervision is the ingredient

    Field+FS reaches 0.86 firm / 0.52 gentle; force supervision alone already modulates grip.

Ablation / what actually enables force

Touch realizes its value through supervision.

The baseline without tactile input and without force supervision fails completely. Every variant that predicts the six-dimensional fingertip-force target gains force modulation; every variant without it stays near zero under the gentle condition.

With the force loss enabled, the marker-field adapter gives the best firm–gentle trade-off: 0.86 firm / 0.52 gentle, against 0.82 / 0.45 for supervision alone.

Tacinf–Taco · Sim-to-real

Taco.

A simulator that earns its seat next to the real robot.

Taco aligns the workspace, objects, embodiment, cameras, and tactile response to the task — then mixes real teleoperation, simulation replay, and UMI demonstrations into one post-training set. The test is policy-relevant consistency: contact onset, patch persistence, force scale, marker response, action replay.

  • A
    Task-aligned scenes

    3D Gaussian workspace and Seed3D object meshes, with rendering and contact kept separate.

  • B
    Embodiment real2sim

    XArm dynamics, the shared Tacumi gripper, and Taxim + FOTS tactile models in one interface.

  • C
    Mixed-data post-training

    Real seeds, simulated visual and force variation, and UMI spatial variation in one policy.

  • D
    Paired evaluation

    Same tasks, language, controller, and termination; simulation versus the physical robot.

Evidence / paired evaluation

r = 0.845 is the harder claim.

Across eight policy variants spanning two VLA backbones and several tactile configurations, the combined task-and-force score gives r = 0.845 and MMRV = 0.195. Success rate alone gives r = 0.726 and MMRV = 0.060, so simulation preserves both task competence and force-level ordering.

The alignment checks CLIP above 0.75, tactile SSIM above 0.93, and action MAE below 0.074 on the channels the policy actually consumes.

Abstract

Infrastructure,
not a checkpoint.

The short version. The paper carries the protocols, tables, and appendices.

Contact-rich manipulation requires a robot to infer appropriate interaction forces from task context and tactile feedback. We present Tacinf, a modular tactile vision–language–action infrastructure for this setting.

It enables policies to predict robot actions and fingertip-force outputs from task language, vision, and touch; an optional language-conditioned force level can be provided when explicit force regulation is required, while ordinary task instructions need no force adverbs. Tacinf integrates synchronized trajectory replay and tactile simulation (Tabero), policy learning (VTLA), handheld collection with a shared deployment gripper (Tacumi), and task-aligned simulation-to-real evaluation (Taco).

We validate each component — simulation data, benchmark, controller, and Tacumi — and report the first zero-shot transfer of a foundation model fine-tuned on tactile simulation data only to real-robot tasks. Eight paired policy variants reach Pearson r = 0.845 between simulated and real combined task-and-force scores. Teleoperation, simulation, and Tacumi data share one contract for mixed-data scaling.

A

A modular tactile interface

Policies adapt from task language, vision, and touch, with an optional force level when explicit regulation is needed.

B

Evaluation that transfers

A simulation protocol and real-robot alignment procedure test whether tactile policies are measured the same way in both domains.

C

One data contract

A shared-gripper UMI interface unifies teleoperation, simulation, and handheld trajectories for mixed-data scaling.
