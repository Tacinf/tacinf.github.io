Tacinf–Tacumi · Hardware and collection

Tacumi.

A handheld interface that records the same gripper the robot will deploy.

The operator’s grip drives a QDD parallel gripper; a servo renders the slave motor’s torque back to the hand. Xense tactile fingertips and a wrist camera record contact as it happens. On the robot, the same gripper, sensors, and motor mount unchanged.

Tabero · ICML 2026 / Benchmark + force-aware VTLA

Tabero.

Learning gentle manipulation with closed-loop force feedback from vision, touch, and language.

Tabero turns open-source manipulation trajectories into vision–tactile–language tasks in Isaac Lab with Taxim/FOTS. Its force-aware VTLA pairs a decoupled force–position interface with a fixed hybrid controller, cutting average grip force by over 70% under gentle instructions while preserving task success.

Tacinf–VTLA · Policy

Tacinf–VTLA.

Vision–language–action, with force as a first-class output.

A modular tactile interface for foundation VLA models. Marker-field, tactile-image, and 6-D force adapters feed a shared backbone; the policy predicts pose and fingertip-force setpoints, and a hybrid controller tracks both. Force is decoupled from position, so language can set how hard to hold without rewriting the motion.

Tacinf–Taco · Sim-to-real

Taco.

A simulator that earns its seat next to the real robot.

Taco aligns the workspace, objects, embodiment, cameras, and tactile response to the task — then mixes real teleoperation, simulation replay, and UMI demonstrations into one post-training set. The test is policy-relevant consistency: contact onset, patch persistence, force scale, marker response, action replay.

Evaluation in the paper ↗ Tabero benchmark ↗

Evidence / paired evaluation

r = 0.845 is the harder claim.

Across eight policy variants spanning two VLA backbones and several tactile configurations, the combined task-and-force score gives r = 0.845 and MMRV = 0.195. Success rate alone gives r = 0.726 and MMRV = 0.060, so simulation preserves both task competence and force-level ordering.

The alignment checks CLIP above 0.75, tactile SSIM above 0.93, and action MAE below 0.074 on the channels the policy actually consumes.
